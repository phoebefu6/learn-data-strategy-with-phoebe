Part 1 · covers centralised, hub-and-spoke and federated teams

Three shapes for one data team

5 min live

Every data operating model answers two questions: where do the data people sit, and who decides what they work on? A centralised team keeps both in one place. A federated model puts both in the business domains and keeps only the standards in the middle. Hub-and-spoke splits them: a central hub owns the shared foundations and the standards, and analysts embedded in each domain work on that domain's priorities. None of the three is right in general; each optimises one thing and breaks in a recognisable way.

LiveWhat each shape is for, and the sentence you hear when it breaks3 min▶

Centralised is the right first shape when there is almost nothing to share yet: one team, one standard, one place to hire. It breaks when demand outgrows the team, and you hear it as "we put a ticket in three months ago". Federated suits an organisation whose domains are mature and genuinely different; each domain moves at its own speed. It breaks on anything shared, and you hear it as "which customer number is the real one?", which is Keelson's diagnosis word for word.

Hub-and-spoke tries to keep the shared parts central and the priorities local. The hub builds the customer and claims record once; the spokes in claims, fraud and pricing use it for their own decisions. It breaks when the hub keeps doing the spokes' work because it is faster to do it than to teach it, and you hear it as "the central team built our dashboard and now nobody here can change it".

Self-studyChoosing from the diagnosis, not from fashion3 min▶

Read your session 1 diagnosis and ask what it says is broken. If the crux is a shared record that nobody owns, a federated model makes it worse, because it hands the shared record to everyone and therefore to no one. If the crux is that the central team cannot hear the business, centralising further makes it worse. Keelson's diagnosis names both: the customer and claims data cannot be joined, and five pilots each rebuilt the same foundations. That points to a hub for the foundations and spokes close to the decisions.

The shape is also a stage, not a destination. Many organisations start central because there is no one in the domains to federate to, then move people out as the shared foundations become reliable. Write down the condition under which you would change shape, so the next change is a decision rather than a reorganisation by mood. Redesigning the wider organisation for AI is a different job, covered in Leadership and AI ↗.

Real world

The centre of excellence that became a queue. A familiar pattern in any organisation: a central analytics team is set up to "serve the business", delivers its first projects brilliantly, and two years later has a backlog nobody can see the end of. The business starts hiring its own analysts quietly, each building a private customer list. Without anyone deciding it, the organisation has drifted from centralised to an unplanned federation, with none of the standards a planned one would have kept.

Part 2 · covers the CDO reporting line

Where the CDO reports

5 min live

The reporting line decides which conversations the CDO is in by default, and therefore which kind of value the data function is pulled toward. There are four common options: the CEO, the COO, the CFO, or the CIO or CTO. Each buys something real and costs something real. A single survey figure about CEO reporting is quoted everywhere, and it is worth reading carefully before anyone uses it as an argument.

LiveReading the 36 percent honestly3 min▶

Gartner's 2025 CDAO Agenda Survey (504 respondents, fielded September to November 2024, reported) found 36 percent of chief data and analytics officers report to the CEO, up from 21 percent in the 2024 survey. The same release reported that 70 percent hold primary responsibility for AI strategy and operating model. The press releases could not be opened at source, so these are reported figures, consistent across trade coverage.

What the number can carry: reporting to the CEO is becoming more common among the people who answer Gartner surveys. What it cannot carry: that CEO reporting works better, or that you should ask for it. A survey of who reports where says nothing about outcomes, and most respondents still report elsewhere. If you use the figure in a conversation with your sponsor, use it as "this is moving", never as "this is the norm".

Self-studyTenure numbers, and where the role's own research lives3 min▶

Talks about the CDO role often open with an average tenure, usually short and alarming. Several such numbers circulate. None that could be traced for this page had a readable method; each was a vendor or survey claim with no description of who was asked or how tenure was counted. Treat them the way session 1 treats "80 percent of data projects fail": a story people tell, not evidence, and never a line in a board paper.

The role does have a long-running research community. The MIT Chief Data Officer and Information Quality programme grew out of MIT's Total Data Quality Management programme (1988), took the CDOIQ name in 2008, and runs an annual symposium chaired by Richard Wang (reported). It is a useful place to hear how peers set up their teams; read what is presented there as practitioner experience, not as measured findings.

Real world

Keelson's line, for now. Maya reports to Tom, the COO (constructed). That is a fine line for CDQ and CFT, which are operational, and a risk for anything offensive later, such as pricing. Maya writes the risk down with a trigger: "If the second wave of bets is mainly pricing and growth, we revisit the line with the CEO." A reporting line chosen with a stated trigger is a decision; one inherited from whoever sponsored the hire is an accident.

Part 3 · covers domain ownership and minimum governance

Just enough governance to ship

5 min live

DAMA-DMBOK, the second edition of DAMA International's body of knowledge (2017), organises data management into 11 knowledge areas with data governance at the centre of the wheel (reported). It is a map of everything a mature function eventually does, and a new CDO who tries to stand up all of it before shipping anything will ship nothing. Three bets need three things: the critical data elements each bet depends on, one named owner for each bet's data, and one forum where disagreements get decided.

LiveThe three-part minimum3 min▶

Critical data elements. For each bet, list the handful of fields it cannot work without, and define each one in a sentence the owner signs. CDQ needs the loss amount captured at first notice, not at settlement; FTR needs one customer ID per person. Nine elements for three bets is a list a claims team can actually keep. A data dictionary of every field in the policy system is not.

One owner per bet's data. A business person, not the CDO, who answers for the definition and quality of those elements, because they own the decision the data feeds. One decision forum. Somewhere a definition dispute ends: Priya wants "claim opened" to mean the first call, Ben wants it to mean the first reserve. The forum decides, records why, and moves on. Without it the dispute ends in whichever team builds first.

Self-studyThe DAMA wheel, as a map rather than a plan4 min▶

DAMA-DMBOK (2nd edition, 2017) lists 11 knowledge areas around governance at the centre: data governance, architecture, modelling and design, storage and operations, security, integration and interoperability, documents and content, reference and master data, warehousing and business intelligence, metadata, and data quality (reported). Some descriptions count 10, because the wheel graphic sets governance apart from the other ten. Either count is the same content.

Use the wheel to check what your minimum leaves out on purpose, not as a list to staff. Keelson's three bets touch data quality (CDQ), reference and master data (the customer ID), and metadata (the element definitions); everything else waits until a bet needs it. Policies, stewardship programmes, metadata tooling and maturity models are real work, taught in depth in Data Governance ↗. Tonight's job is the smallest slice that lets the bets start.

Real world

The governance programme with no customer. A familiar pattern in any organisation: a governance council is chartered, a policy framework is drafted across every knowledge area, and stewards are named for domains no project is touching. A year in, attendance falls, because nothing on the agenda is blocking anyone's work. Governance tied to three live bets has the opposite problem: the agenda writes itself, because every meeting has a decision someone is waiting for.

Build-along

Keelson's model and who does what

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Tonight's page of Keelson's strategy answers "who delivers". Maya picks a team shape from the diagnosis, records the reporting line with its trigger, names the owners, and writes a RACI for CDQ, FTR and CFT: R does the work, A answers for the outcome (exactly one per row), C is consulted before, I is told after. Every name and letter is constructed; if you are working on your own organisation, your letters will differ, and they should.

Choose the shape from the diagnosis. Score each shape against the two halves of Keelson's crux: does it fix the shared record, and does it keep the work close to the decisions?

Record the reporting line and its trigger. One line for where Maya reports today, one for the condition that would reopen it. The trigger is what makes it a decision rather than an inheritance.

List the critical data elements per bet. Three per bet is plenty. Mark any element two bets share, because that is where a definition fight will start.

★ Step 3 · critical data elements (constructed)CDQ loss amount at first notice (shared with CFT) claim status and its date policy number on the claim FTR one customer ID per person (shared with CFT) prior claims per customer first-notice description CFT loss amount at first notice claim type and cover customer ID on the claim

Name one owner for each bet's data. The owner is the business person whose decision the data feeds. If two people both claim it, that is the forum's first agenda item, not a reason to name both.

★ Step 4 · ownersCDQ Priya Natarajan, head of claims (her handlers capture the amount at first notice) FTR Elena Ruiz, chief risk officer (fraud referral decisions sit in her function) CFT Priya Natarajan, head of claims (fast-track is a claims-handling decision)

Fill the RACI, then check it. Exactly one A per row. The CDO's team is usually R on foundations and rarely A on a business outcome; if Maya is A on a bet, ask whose decision it really is.

★ Step 5 · RACI with three checks Maya Tom Priya Ben Elena CDQ R I A C C FTR R I C I A CFT R I A C I Check 1: one A per row? yes Check 2: is the CDO ever A? no Check 3: is everyone who signs a number at least C or I? yes (Ben on reserves)

Charter the forum in four lines. Who chairs, who comes, how often, and what it decides. Anything it does not decide goes back to the owner.

★ Step 6 · decision forum charterChair: Tom Reyes (COO). Run by: Maya Okafor. Members: the three bet owners; Ben for anything touching reserves. Cadence: monthly, 60 minutes, plus ad hoc when a bet is blocked. Decides: definitions of the nine elements, conflicts between bets, and when a bet is ready to ship.

Test the RACI on a real disagreement. Before Maya circulates the page, she runs one live dispute through it: Priya and Ben disagree on when a claim counts as opened. The RACI says Priya is A for CDQ and Ben is C, so Priya decides after hearing Ben, and the forum records the definition. If the document cannot settle the first real argument, rewrite it before anyone relies on it.

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 4 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Keelson's diagnosis says the customer record cannot be joined and every pilot rebuilt the same foundations. Which shape follows from it?

Federated hands the shared record to everyone and so to no one, which is the problem Keelson already has. Fully centralised fixes the record but puts claims and fraud in a queue. A peer's shape answers a peer's diagnosis, not yours.

2 · A board member says "36 percent of CDAOs now report to the CEO, so ours should too." What is the honest reply?

The figure has a real source, Gartner's 2025 CDAO Agenda Survey, so it is not folklore; it is reported because the release could not be opened. A survey of where people report measures prevalence, not results. Choose the line from your bets, and write down what would make you change it.

3 · What is the minimum governance that lets three bets ship?

The wheel is a map of what a mature function eventually does, not a precondition. A full catalogue is work no bet is waiting for. The owner should be the person whose decision the data feeds, which is rarely the CDO.
