Part 1 · covers value, feasibility and data readiness

Three lenses, one number, and a gate

6 min live

Every bet gets three parts between 0 and 1. Value is the band rescaled. Feasibility falls with effort and with regulatory risk. Readiness is the readiness score over 100. The weighted average, times 100, is the bet's score. Before any of that, a gate: a bet whose readiness is under 40 is not eligible this year, because its data does not exist yet. The rule is printed on the widget and the Python reference that checked every number on this page runs the same rule.

LiveWhy three lenses, and who sets the weights3 min▶

Value alone picks prizes. Feasibility alone picks chores. Readiness is the lens most strategies leave out, and it is the one session 1's diagnosis feeds directly: Keelson cannot trust or join its customer and claims data, so any bet that needs those things early scores low on readiness, whatever its value. The default weights here are value 0.5, feasibility 0.2, readiness 0.3. They are a choice, and the right people to make it are the sponsor and the business owners, in the room, before anyone sees the ranking.

How to run that weighting conversation well, and how to calibrate the value bands themselves, is the method of weighing options taught in the Decision Intelligence course ↗. This session takes the scores as given and asks the CDO's question: which three, first.

Self-studyWhy readiness is a gate as well as a weight3 min▶

A weight lets a big enough prize outvote missing data. With readiness only weighted, a bet whose data does not exist can still rank first if its value is high, and the strategy then promises something the organisation cannot start. The gate says plainly: under 40, the first job is to create the data, and that job is a different bet. At Keelson, SEV (claim severity at first notice) cannot be built until claims capture amounts early, which is part of what CDQ, fixing claims data at source, delivers.

The threshold of 40 is constructed and arbitrary. Moving it changes which bets are eligible, which is a decision to make out loud. The break button on the bench removes the gate and the readiness weight together, so you can see what the pick looks like when nobody asks whether the data exists.

Build-along · the bench

Twenty-four bets, five rungs, two ways to get it wrong

★ 26 min · the main event

The bench opens on rung 1. Work down the rungs in order and, before moving on, say out loud what the new constraint removed. Then try the anti-lever and the break. Move the sliders whenever you want to argue with a weight, a budget or the team's capacity; every number is recomputed in this page, and Reset brings back the defaults the text below quotes.

Read it in this order. At the defaults, rung 1 picks PEL, FNW and PHO: value 28, cost 2,110 against a budget of 1,100, and all three under the readiness gate. Rung 3 picks CFT, FTR and SUB, which fits the money but leaves CFT and SUB without CDQ, the claims fix they depend on. Rung 4's exact search over 283 valid sets picks CDQ, FTR and CFT: value 21, cost 960, 21 team-months, two offense and one defense.

Part 2 · covers what the ladder shows, and why quick wins mislead

Value captured falls, and that is the point

6 min live

Each rung adds one piece of reality, and the value on the slide drops: 28 for the first two rungs, 23 with readiness, 21 for the exact pick. That drop is not the strategy getting worse. The first two picks were never deliverable, and the third could not start without a bet it left out. The anti-lever is the one rung that is genuinely worse.

LiveQuick wins first, priced3 min▶

Pressure to show something in 90 days pushes every new CDO toward the cheapest, fastest items. On the bench that rule picks KPI, BRK and LTR: an executive dashboard refresh, a broker dashboard and a generative AI letter-drafting pilot. Value captured is 10 against 21 for the exact set, it uses 6 of the 24 team-months, and LTR has no usable data at all: past letters are scanned PDFs in a legacy archive.

The deeper cost is that none of the three touches the diagnosis. In a year Keelson would have two refreshed dashboards and still could not join a customer or see a claim amount early. Quick wins are fine as a side dish, especially to build trust in month one. As the first three bets they spend the credibility of the new role on work that leaves the crux exactly where it was.

LiveExact search against greedy picking3 min▶

Greedy is how most portfolios are built: take the best-scoring bet that fits, then the next, until the money runs out. At the defaults greedy picks FTR, RET and CAT, scoring 196.7 against the exact set's 197.0. The gap is small, and greedy even carries one more value band (22 against 21). What greedy cannot do is take CFT, the highest-scoring eligible bet, because CFT needs CDQ and greedy never chose CDQ first; on its own CDQ scores too low to be picked early.

The exact search tries every valid set of three, 283 of them at the defaults, so it sees that CDQ plus CFT together beat what greedy found. With 24 bets that takes milliseconds. The lesson is not the 0.3 points. It is that foundations like CDQ rarely win a one-at-a-time ranking and often belong in the best set, which is why a CDO should score sets, not items.

Real world

The dashboard year. A familiar pattern in any organisation: a new data leader ships three visible dashboards in the first quarter, is praised, and is asked a year later why nothing structural has changed. The dashboards were real wins. They were chosen because they were fast, not because they moved the diagnosis, and the bench shows the same trade in numbers.

Part 3 · covers rank stability and what to tell the board

How sure are you? Tell the board the range

6 min live

The weights were agreed in a room and could easily have been a little different. Rung 5 asks what happens if they were. It draws each weight 500 times, from 20 percent below to 20 percent above its value, with a fixed seed, and reruns the exact search each time. At the defaults the exact set CDQ, FTR and CFT comes back as the same set of three in 173 of 500 draws, 34.6 percent.

LiveTurning 173 of 500 into a board sentence3 min▶

34.6 percent sounds weak until you read the bars. FTR, fraud triage at first notice, is in the exact set in 352 of 500 draws. The other two slots split between two pairs: CDQ with CFT (the claims fix and the fast-track it enables, 321 each) or CAT with RET (the catastrophe map and renewal retention, 179 each). That is a clear message: one bet survives any reasonable weighting, and the second and third are a judgement between fixing claims and protecting the book.

The sentence for the board: "Fraud triage is first under any weighting we tried. For the other two, we recommend the claims fix and fast-track, because they also answer the diagnosis; the alternative pair scores almost as well and does not." A point estimate would have hidden the judgement; the range puts it on the table where the board can own it.

Self-studyWhat the bench cannot tell you3 min▶

Every row is constructed, so no total on the bench is a forecast: value bands are not money, and a set's score is not a return. The bench shows how the order of bets responds to rules and limits. Your own portfolio will have different bands, different dependencies and different readiness, and the method transfers while the numbers do not.

The model also leaves things out on purpose: bets interact beyond simple dependencies, risk is reduced to a 1-3 penalty, and three is a fixed count. Building the business case for each bet, with costs and benefits in money, is planned for the business cases and ROI course, which is not yet live. Placing the chosen bets on the offense-defense dial from session 2 is the widget's "Offense : defense" tile: two offense and one defense for the exact set.

Rerun it with the people who set the weights. Put the bench on the projector with the sponsor and the business owners, move one weight each, and let them watch which bets hold. People defend a portfolio they have seen survive their own objections.

Homework

Before session 4

30 min
  1. Score your own longlist. Ten candidate bets, each with a value band, effort, cost, readiness and dependencies, in a spreadsheet. Note who agreed each value band.
  2. Find your gate. Which bets would fail a readiness gate of 40, and what bet would create the missing data?
  3. Price your quick wins. Pick your three fastest items and write which part of your diagnosis each one changes. If the answer is none for all three, they are not your first bets.
  4. Write your board sentence. One bet that survives any weighting, and the judgement call for the other two.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 3 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Ranking by value alone picks PEL, FNW and PHO at Keelson. Why is that pick unusable?

Value alone ignores money, team, data and dependencies, so its pick cannot start. A is wrong in fact (all three are offense), and B misreads the point: ranking by value is fine as one lens among three.

2 · Greedy picking misses CFT, the best-scoring eligible bet. Why?

CFT costs 360 and has readiness 68, so it passes on money and data. It needs CDQ, which scores too low to be taken early one at a time. Exact search scores whole sets and finds that CDQ plus CFT together win.

3 · The exact set survives as the same set in 173 of 500 weight draws. What should Maya tell the board?

The draws measure how sensitive the ORDER is to the weights, not a probability of success, which rules out C. FTR is in 352 of 500; the rest splits between two pairs. Naming the judgement lets the board own it.
