Part 1 · covers the UK National Data Strategy and the US Federal Data Strategy

Two national strategies, read as worked material

5 min live

National data strategies are the largest published examples of the document Maya is writing, and they are free to read. Read them for structure, not for content to copy: lay each one against Rumelt's kernel (reported) and ask which part is diagnosis, which is guiding policy, and which is action. The mapping below is our reading of each document's structure, not a label either government uses.

LiveThe UK strategy: four pillars, five missions3 min▶

The UK National Data Strategy was published by the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport on 9 September 2020, with the page updated on 9 December 2020. It rests on four pillars: data foundations, data skills, data availability and responsible data. It sets five missions: unlocking the value of data across the economy; securing a pro-growth and trusted data regime; transforming government's use of data to drive efficiency and improve public services; ensuring the security and resilience of the infrastructure on which data relies; and championing the international flow of data.

Read against the kernel, the pillars behave like a guiding policy: they say what kind of work counts, and by implication what does not. The missions behave like actions, though at national scale each one is a programme in itself. Notice the first pillar. "Data foundations" sits beside the other three as an equal, which is the national version of Maya's policy line: build the shared records first, and fund only what uses them. A pillar list cannot tell you which one comes first; a diagnosis can. That is the question to put to any published strategy, and to your own.

Self-studyThe US strategy: principles, practices, plans4 min▶

The US Federal Data Strategy, as its own overview page sets it out, has a mission statement, 10 principles in three groups (ethical governance, conscious design, learning culture), 40 practices in three groups (culture, practices 1 to 10; governing, managing and protecting data, 11 to 26; efficient and appropriate use, 27 to 40), and annual action plans, of which 2020 and 2021 are listed. It was issued as OMB memorandum M-19-18, "Federal Data Strategy - A Framework for Consistency", on 4 June 2019, with a ten-year vision (reported: the memorandum itself was not opened for this page).

The useful part for a CDO is the layering. Principles change rarely, practices are a stable menu, and the action plan is the only layer that commits to doing specific things in a specific year. That is a sound shape for a company too: a one-page strategy that holds for two or three years, and a roadmap that is rewritten each period. One more lesson sits on the site itself, which says it is being retired to resources.data.gov. Strategies have a shelf life; write yours expecting the action layer to be replaced and the diagnosis to be checked again.

Real world

Two governments, the same missing first sentence. Both national documents are well organised, public and written by people who knew the subject. Neither leads, in the parts set out above, with a single sentence that says what is going on and why it matters most. At national scale that may be the right choice: a government strategy has to hold many departments together. A company strategy has a narrower job, and a board will ask Maya the question the pillars cannot answer: of all this, what comes first, and why?

Part 2 · covers Singapore's Government Data Strategy and the Digital Government Blueprint

Singapore, and where a data strategy lives

5 min live

If you search for Singapore's national data strategy, you will not find a document with that title. The Smart Nation "frameworks and blueprints" page lists the Digital Government Blueprint (2020 edition), the Smart Nation 2.0 Report (2024), the National AI Strategy 2.0 (2023) and others, and none of them is titled a data strategy. Singapore's Government Data Strategy was launched in 2018 inside the Digital Government Blueprint, first published in June 2018 (reported, from secondary sources). The data strategy is a part of something larger, not a document on its own.

LiveWhat the missing title teaches3 min▶

A search that finds no "national data strategy" for Singapore does not mean Singapore has no data strategy. It means the data strategy was written as part of the government's digital plan, beside the services it was meant to improve. That placement has a practical effect a new CDO can use: a data strategy inside the business plan is funded, reviewed and replaced on the business plan's cycle, so it cannot quietly drift away from the decisions it serves.

The alternative, a standalone data strategy, is easier to write and easier to ignore. It gets its own launch, its own deck and its own review, and a year later nobody outside the data team can name a decision it changed. For Keelson, Maya asks Tom for one page inside the business plan Tom takes to the board, not a separate volume. Her three bets then appear as lines in the plan's own budget, which is also where session 5's gates are read.

Self-studyHow sure we are about Singapore2 min▶

Two different tiers sit in Part 2. The list of documents on the Smart Nation frameworks and blueprints page, and the fact that none is titled a data strategy, were read on that page. The 2018 launch of the Government Data Strategy inside the first Digital Government Blueprint comes from secondary reporting (GovInsider and the Civil Service College's Ethos), so it is stated here as reported. If you cite it in a board paper, cite it the same way, or open the Blueprint yourself first.

The broader lesson does not depend on the date. Wherever a data strategy is written, the test is the same one from session 1: does it name what is going on, and does it rule anything out? A chapter that does both is worth more than a standalone volume that does neither.

Real world

The strategy with its own launch party. A familiar pattern in any organisation: the data strategy is launched separately, with its own branding and town hall, while the business plan is written down the corridor on a different calendar. When budgets are set, the business plan wins, because it is the document finance reads. Putting the data strategy inside the business plan from the start avoids the contest rather than winning it.

Part 3 · covers sequencing, risks and a comms cadence for the first 100 days

A hundred days, in order

5 min live

A one-page strategy says what and why. The 100-day roadmap says in what order, and the order is not a matter of taste: it follows the dependencies the bench found in session 3 and the gates written in session 5. CFT cannot start spending until CDQ's intake record holds, so CDQ starts on day 1 and CFT's money waits. The roadmap's other two jobs are to name the risks out loud and to set a rhythm of communication that makes progress visible before the lagging numbers move.

LiveSequence by dependency, not by appetite3 min▶

Every sponsor wants the visible bet first. At Keelson that is CFT: simple claims settled faster is something a customer notices. The bench in session 3 already said why it cannot go first: greedy ranking could not take CFT before CDQ, and the exact search only reached CDQ, FTR and CFT because it scored them as a set with the dependency inside. The roadmap carries that forward in time. CDQ starts on day 1 on its central line, FTR starts in parallel because it does not wait on CDQ, and CFT's first tranche is released only when its gate reads CDQ's intake record as holding.

That ordering also gives Tom something to show early. FTR's leading signal, the share of claims scored at first notice, can move within weeks, so the first monthly forum has a number on the table while CDQ is still underneath the floorboards. Plan the visible early signal deliberately; do not reorder the foundation to get one. The delivery mechanics, plans, dependencies and status reporting belong to the Data PMO course ↗.

Self-studyFive risks to name on day 14 min▶

A risk written into the roadmap is a decision made in advance. A risk discovered at the board is an excuse. For Keelson, five risks follow directly from the earlier pages (all constructed):

  • CDQ slips. CFT waits longer. The gate already says what happens: CFT's money stays unspent, not reallocated, so nothing has to be renegotiated.
  • The definition fight. Priya and Ben disagree on when a claim counts as opened. Session 4's RACI and forum settle it: Priya decides for CDQ after hearing Ben, and the forum records the definition.
  • FTR scores, nobody uses them. The leading metric rises while referrals do not change. Session 5's wrong-way reading catches it: referral rate up and confirmed share down means the score is sending noise.
  • The range is forgotten. The same three bets survived 173 of 500 draws, with FTR in 352. Slots two and three are a judgement, and the roadmap should say so before someone else does.
  • The sponsor moves. Maya reports to Tom. If Tom leaves or the next wave of bets is mostly pricing and growth, session 4's trigger says to revisit the reporting line with the CEO.
Real world

The strategy that went quiet. A familiar pattern in any organisation: the strategy is approved with real enthusiasm, the team disappears into building foundations, and nothing is said for months because there is nothing finished to show. By the time there is, the sponsor has been asked twice what the money bought and has stopped defending it. A weekly note of three lines (what moved, what is blocked, what we need) and a monthly forum with one leading number cost little and remove the silence that loses sponsors.

Build-along

Keelson's strategy on one page

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Maya folds five pages into one and adds a 100-day roadmap behind it. Nothing new is invented tonight: every line below comes from a page built in sessions 1 to 5, which is the test of a coherent strategy. Everything is constructed. If you have your own pages, assemble them as you go; your page will differ, and every line on it should still trace to your diagnosis.

Lay out the five pages you already have. One line each, copied, not rewritten. If a page is missing, that is the gap to close before the board, not something to paper over on the summary.

★ Step 1 · the inputs (constructed)Session 1 diagnosis paragraph, and one policy line Session 2 position on the dial, trigger, ambition statement Session 3 three bets from the bench, with the range Session 4 team shape, reporting line, RACI, forum charter Session 5 funding model per bet, gates, six metrics

Write the kernel block first. Diagnosis in two sentences, then the policy, then the dial with its trigger. These are the lines a board member should be able to repeat after one reading.

★ Step 2 · the kernelDiagnosis Keelson cannot trust or join its customer and claims data, so every win stays a one-off: five pilots proved value and none scaled. Policy Build the shared customer and early claim record first, and fund only the bets that use them. Dial Lean defense now; move toward balance when the claims total reconciles two quarters running, with no manual rebuild.

Add the coherent actions with their range. Three bets, what each is, the dependency, and how sure you are. The range is not a weakness on the page; it is the line that tells the board which choices are firm and which are judgement.

★ Step 3 · three betsCDQ claims data quality fixed at source (defense) FTR fraud triage score at first notice (offense) CFT claims fast-track for simple claims (offense) CFT needs CDQ. Fit 960 of 1,100 (thousands, constructed); 21 of 24 team-months Range same three in 173 of 500 draws; FTR in 352. FTR is firm; CDQ + CFT answer the diagnosis.

Say who delivers and how it is paid for. Two short blocks. Keep only what a board member would ask about: the shape, the line, the owners, the money, and the stop rule.

★ Step 4 · who and how paidWho Hub-and-spoke: Maya's hub builds the customer and claims record once; analysts sit in claims and fraud. Maya reports to Tom (COO). Owners CDQ Priya (A) · FTR Elena (A) · CFT Priya (A) Forum monthly, chaired by Tom, run by Maya Funding CDQ central; FTR staged; CFT staged, gated on CDQ's intake record. 140 (thousands) and 3 team-months held back. Stop a gate read as "no" returns that tranche

Add the signals, one line per bet. Leading first, because that is what the board will see moving in the first 100 days; lagging second, with the owner who answers for it.

★ Step 5 · signalsCDQ leading share of new claims keyed complete at first notice lagging hand reserve rebuilds per quarter Ben, Elena FTR leading share of claims scored at first notice lagging referral rate, confirmed share Priya CFT leading share of simple claims fast-tracked lagging days, first notice to settlement Priya

Write the 100-day roadmap behind the page. Three blocks of days, each with what starts, which gate is read, and what is said to whom. The days are constructed; set yours from your own gates.

★ Step 6 · the first 100 days (constructed)Days 1-30 CDQ starts on its central line. FTR stage 1 goes live. Forum chartered; first meeting at day 30. Weekly 3-line note to Tom starts week 1. Days 31-60 FTR gate 1 read at day 45: continue, fix adoption, or stop and return the rest. Forum at day 60: first leading numbers. Days 61-100 CFT gate 0 read at day 70: tranche 1 only if CDQ's intake record holds. Forum at day 90. Board at day 100: three leading signals, one honest range.

Run the one-page tests before it leaves your desk. If any answer is no, fix the page, not the test.

★ Step 7 · four tests1. Does every line trace to a session's page? yes: see the left margin of the figure 2. Does it rule something out? yes: anything needing a clean customer or an early claim amount waits for CDQ 3. Could Tom repeat the diagnosis after one reading? read it to him and ask 4. Does it fit on one page, in the business plan? yes, if the roadmap sits behind it, not on it

Read the page aloud in two minutes. Maya reads the finished page to Tom at normal speed, without slides. If it runs over two minutes, a block is carrying detail that belongs in its session page, and it goes back there. The full pages from sessions 1 to 5 travel with the one-pager as its appendix, so nothing is lost by cutting.

Homework

Before your own day one

30 min
  1. Read one national strategy for structure. Open the UK or US document and mark which parts are diagnosis, policy and action. Write down the sentence you would add to make the diagnosis explicit.
  2. Decide where your strategy lives. Name the document your organisation's leadership already reads, usually the business plan, and ask its owner for one page inside it.
  3. Assemble your own one-pager. Six blocks, each traced to the work behind it. Mark any block with nothing behind it; that is your next conversation.
  4. Draft your first 100 days. Three blocks of days, the gates read in each, and the comms rhythm: who hears what, weekly, monthly, and at the board.
Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 6 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · Singapore has no document titled a national data strategy. What is the honest reading?

The Smart Nation page lists several blueprints and none is titled a data strategy; secondary sources report the Government Data Strategy was launched inside the Digital Government Blueprint. A and D confuse a title with a strategy, and C claims a replacement nobody has shown.

2 · In the US Federal Data Strategy, which layer commits to specific work in a specific year?

Principles and practices are the stable layers; the action plan is the one replaced each period. That is the shape to copy: a one-page strategy that holds for years and a roadmap rewritten each period.

3 · On day 70, CDQ's intake record is not yet holding. What happens to CFT's first tranche?

Session 5 wrote CFT's gate before any money moved: on a no, CFT's money stays unspent, not reallocated. Because the rule was agreed in advance, a delay is the plan working, and nothing has to be renegotiated in front of the board.
